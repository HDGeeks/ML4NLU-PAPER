SECTION 4.1 — Quantitative Overview
----------------------------------------------------------------------

ISSUE 1: Missing models
You mention "mBERT, XLM-R, and XLM-V" — the study has FIVE models:
mBERT, XLM-R-base, XLM-R-large, XLM-V-base, and mDeBERTa-v3-base.
XLM-R-large and mDeBERTa are completely absent from your analysis.
mDeBERTa is the central finding of the entire paper.

ISSUE 2: "across all 12 layers"
Models do not all have 12 layers. mBERT, XLM-R-base, XLM-V, and
mDeBERTa each produce 13 hidden states (layers 0-12). XLM-R-large
produces 25 hidden states (layers 0-24). The layer count varies by model.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

ISSUE 3: "Layers 1-5 range from -0.12 to +0.25" — FABRICATED
The CSVs show this is not true. Examples from actual data:
  - XLM-R-base Tigrigna, layer 2: -0.617
  - XLM-R-base Tigrigna, layer 5: +0.516
  - XLM-R-base Arabic, layer 2: -0.962
The range in early layers is much wider than -0.12 to +0.25.
Do not cite a numerical range that is not in the output files.

ISSUE 4: "Arabic engineer (muhandis) reached +5.80 at Layer 11"
This number does not appear in any CSV. The closest real values are:
  - XLM-R-base: مهندس peak = +3.86 at layer 9
  - XLM-R-large: مهندس peak = +5.67 at layer 14 (not layer 11)
  - XLM-V-base: مهندس peak = +3.30 at layer 9
The +5.80 figure is wrong. The peak for XLM-R-large is +5.67 at layer 14.

ISSUE 5: "peak in the penultimate layers" / "starting at Layer 8"
Peak layers differ by model and language:
  - XLM-R-base: peaks at layer 9 (most languages)

  - XLM-V-base: peaks at layer 11
There is no universal "Layer 8 explosion." For XLM-R-large, the buildup
continues all the way to layers 14 and 23.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 4.3 — The Tigrigna Paradox
----------------------------------------------------------------------

ISSUE 6: "mBERT Tigrigna mean Proj = -6.42 at Layer 11"
The number -6.42 is technically in the CSV (layer 11 mean = -6.418).
HOWEVER, citing this number without its critical context is a serious
error. mBERT maps ALL Tigrigna tokens to [UNK] because Tigrigna uses
Ge'ez script and is absent from mBERT's WordPiece vocabulary. The entire
mBERT Tigrigna output is measuring [UNK] token bias, not profession bias.
It is not a valid data point and the paper explicitly places it in an
appendix as a degenerate case. You must not use it as a comparable
result alongside XLM-V.

ISSUE 7: The "Polarity Paradox" is built on a false comparison
You compare mBERT (female-leaning, -6.42) with XLM-V (male-leaning, +2.63)
and conclude this is a "tokenization noise" effect. This is wrong on two counts:
  (a) mBERT's value is [UNK] noise, not a real Tigrigna measurement.
  (b) The actual polarity reversal finding is mDeBERTa (28 of 30 professions
      female-leaning, peak = -1.84), which you do not mention at all.
      mDeBERTa uses SentencePiece and tokenizes Tigrigna correctly.
      The reversal is therefore NOT tokenization noise — it is an architecture
      effect from Disentangled Attention.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 4.4 — Spearman Stability
----------------------------------------------------------------------

ISSUE 8: "rho ≈ 0.99"
The global minimum Spearman rho across all valid models, layers, and
languages is 0.964 (XLM-R-base, Arabic, layer 0). While most values
are close to 1.0, reporting "≈ 0.99" overstates the floor.
The correct statement is rho >= 0.964.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 4.5 — Layer 12 Collapse
----------------------------------------------------------------------

ISSUE 9: "A recurring pattern across EVERY model and language"
The collapse at layer 12 is NOT uniform. XLM-R-base layer 12 is near
zero for most languages (Tigrigna: +0.04, Spanish: close to zero) — a
gentle decay, not a crash. The dramatic collapse is specific to:
  - XLM-V-base (Tigrigna layer 12: extreme values)
  - mDeBERTa-v3-base (layer 12: values exceeding -17 and +12 individually)
Saying "every model" is inaccurate.

ISSUE 10: "After reaching maximum at Layer 11"
As noted in Issue 5, XLM-R-large peaks at layers 14 and 23, not 11.
The Layer 12 "crash" also only applies to 13-layer models. In XLM-R-large
the final layer is 24, and the decay begins much earlier.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 5.2 — CRITICAL MISINTERPRETATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

ISSUE 11: "Architecture determines Depth, Data determines Direction"
This conclusion directly contradicts the main finding of the paper.
The study's primary contribution is that mDeBERTa-v3-base, trained on
comparable multilingual data to XLM-R, produces a COMPLETE direction
reversal — 28 of 30 Tigrigna professions are female-leaning, versus
male-leaning in XLM-R. The ONLY architectural difference is Disentangled
Attention (mDeBERTa) vs standard multi-head attention (XLM-R).

This means architecture determines BOTH depth AND direction. Data alone
does not determine direction. Your conclusion gets this backwards and
dismisses the central finding of the paper.

Correction: Architecture (specifically attention mechanism design)
determines both the depth of bias encoding AND the direction of bias.
This is demonstrated by the mDeBERTa reversal, confirmed across all
three languages (Arabic: -3.04, Tigrigna: -1.84, Spanish: -0.62).

----------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 5.4 — XLM-V Vocabulary
----------------------------------------------------------------------

ISSUE 12: "XLM-V showed more civilized bias trajectories for Tigrigna"
This is not supported by the data. XLM-V-base has the most extreme
individual profession values at layer 12 (reaching values in the range
of -17 to +18 for individual terms). The trajectory is anything but
"civilized" — the collapse at layer 12 is the most dramatic of all models.

ISSUE 13: "Increasing vocabulary acts as structural de-biasing"
The data does not support this conclusion. XLM-V-base shows a Tigrigna
peak projection of +2.63 — strong male-leaning bias. A larger vocabulary
improves tokenization coverage (two fewer professions are lost to
vocab mismatch), but it does NOT reduce the bias magnitude itself.
Calling it "structural de-biasing" is not justified by the results.
