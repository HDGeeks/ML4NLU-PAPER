\begin{abstract}
We investigate gender bias in XLM-RoBERTa-base as reflected in the
contextual representations of Tigrigna profession words, probing all 13
transformer layers using two independent bias estimators.  We find that
the model produces near-static representations for all 30 Tigrigna
professions tested (mean pairwise cosine similarity $> 0.91$), a
consequence of the language's low representation in pretraining data.
Despite this, substantial and consistent male bias is present in the
mid-to-deep transformer layers, peaking at layer~9.
Comparing static embedding bias with contextual bias reveals two
qualitatively distinct mechanisms: embedding-level bias (present before
any attention is applied) and attention-level bias (imposed by the
transformer layers on otherwise neutral tokens).  Both ProjBias and
CosDiff estimators agree near-perfectly across all layers
(Spearman $\rho \geq 0.992$, $p < 10^{-26}$).
\end{abstract}
